Refract design system
A dark room with
lit glass in it.
The library is the only saturated thing on screen. Chrome is transparent, borrowing colour from whatever is behind it. One accent hue lights the edges. The theme raises its voice in exactly one circumstance: a rating high enough to have earned it.
Everything that follows is that sentence, made specific enough to argue with. Full text: DESIGN_SYSTEM.md in the repo root.
The principles
NINE
P1
The image is the subject.
Chrome never out-saturates content. If a control is more colourful than the thumbnail next to it, the control is wrong.
P2
One accent, one hue, never assumed.
Eight presets ship, plus light mode, plus a custom override. A design that reads correctly only in orange is broken in seven other places.
P3
Emphasis is earned, not applied.
Visual intensity is a function of data. A card glows because it is rated 9.8, not because glowing looks good.
P4
Restyle, never reimplement.
Stash owns behaviour and state. Refract owns presentation. The moment Refract owns a piece of state, it owns every bug in it forever.
P5
Degrade in tiers, not cliffs.
Every effect has a defined behaviour in dark, light, lite, reduced motion and reduced transparency. "It disappears" is valid. "Nobody checked" is not.
P6
Reachable beats visible.
Moving a control behind a disclosure is a design decision. Losing it is not. Nothing Stash could do before may become impossible after.
P7
Space goes to density.
Nothing dense may hide behind an inner scrollbar while whitespace goes unused in the same column. 34 tag chips crushed into 98px above 46.5px of dead space is the canonical failure.
P8
Give people the means. Never claim knowledge the theme does not have.
Refract cannot tell which image is safe for work. So it does not guess. It makes the parts configurable and lets the user express the intent.
P9
Measure the live page. Reading the CSS does not tell you what it does.
A change can be correct in the source and do nothing on screen. Four "obvious" causes of the scroll problem were each ruled out by A/B before the real one was found.
